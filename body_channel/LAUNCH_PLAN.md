## Cadence: 4 long videos in month 1 (one every Wednesday) plus 2 Shorts per video
- **Why 4, not 8:** each script needs 10–15 primary sources and a fact-check pass. Eight a month would mean cutting the research, which is the whole brand. Four also gives YouTube four chances to find the audience, plus an A/B thumbnail test on each.
- **Why 8–10 minutes, not 12+:** the scripts are 1,100–1,400 words (about 8–10 min with pauses). That passes the 8-minute mid-roll mark, and the format is a countdown, which loses tension if it runs long. The top body-science results we found run 3–12 min.
- **Why 2 Shorts per video (the reference channel does 1):** body facts make perfect Shorts ("17 hours awake = 0.05% alcohol", "Lightning gives you a tattoo… then takes it back"). Each one links the long video as "Related video". They feed new viewers into the long videos.
- **Why Wednesday:** the reference channel owns Friday. A mid-week slot avoids competing with our own sister channels and the weekend history and entertainment crowd. Body-science and education videos are watched on weekday evenings and lunch breaks. Uploading on Wednesday gives the algorithm Wednesday to Sunday, the five strongest days, to push the video.
- **Time: 16:00 UTC** (11 AM New York, 4 PM London, 9 PM Pakistan). The video is indexed and getting impressions before the US lunch break and the UK evening.

## What makes them click, and what makes them stay
**Thumbnail rules** (all 8 follow them):
1. One neon "you" on the left, big, on true black. It pops in both dark and light feeds.
2. Two lines of Anton, white plus one hot orange line, 2–3 words each. They open a question and never repeat the title.
3. A vitals chip with an ECG line (for example "HR 0 ● ASYSTOLE"). That's the brand signature, so viewers recognise us in the feed.

**Hook rules** (the first 30 seconds of every script):
1. Start at the worst moment, with the monitor already at CRIT ("Day eleven. 264 hours.").
2. Give one sourced, jaw-drop number in the first 10 seconds.
3. A contradiction or twist promise by second 25 ("90% survive. That's not the good news.").
4. Reset to HOUR 0 / 0 m / T-30 min and promise "hour by hour, organ by organ".

**Retention beats:**
- The monitor changes every 30–60 seconds (a new timer value, the status turns red), so there's always a visual reason to keep watching.
- A twist every ~90 seconds ("Now here's the twist…").
- The outro calls back to the hook, then a 20-second end screen teases the next patient by name.
