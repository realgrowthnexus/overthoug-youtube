# EP01 — What Happens to Your Body If You Stay Awake for 11 Days

_Your Vitals · narration script. Tags like [S3] link each claim to a source below._


## Hook

- `h01` **[DAY 11. NO SLEEP.]** It's day eleven. You haven't slept for two hundred and sixty-four hours. [S1]
- `h02` Your eyes won't focus. Your words come out slurred. Somewhere in your brain, little patches are switching off by themselves, a few seconds at a time, and you don't even notice. [S1, S9]
- `h03` You're seeing things that aren't there. And you're pretty sure the people around you are up to something. [S1]
- `h04` And here's the strange part. Back then, scientists honestly didn't know if this would kill you. In just over a week, a seventeen year old was going to find out, live, in front of the national press. [S2]
- `h05` **[HOUR 0]** So let's do it properly. You. No sleep. Eleven days. Hour by hour, organ by organ, with a vitals monitor that will slowly stop being funny. Let's go.

## Hour 0: The Bet

- `s01` **[San Diego, 1963]** San Diego, December nineteen sixty-three. Two high school boys need a science fair project. Their idea? Stay awake as long as humanly possible. [S2]
- `s02` They flip a coin to decide who has to do it. One of them wins. The other one is Randy Gardner. Today, you are Randy. [S2]
- `s03` **[Record to beat: 260 h]** The record to beat belongs to a radio DJ in Honolulu. Two hundred and sixty hours. Just under eleven days. Easy. You've stayed up late before. [S2]
- `s04` **[No pills. Just Coke.]** The rules are simple. No pills, no stimulants. Your helpers later say you had the occasional Coke, and that's it. [S2]

## Hour 17: Legally Drunk

- `s05` The first night feels like a sleepover. Then the sun comes up, and your body starts sending invoices.
- `s06` **[17 hours = 0.05% alcohol]** At around seventeen to nineteen hours awake, researchers found that people performed on some tests as badly as someone with a blood alcohol level of zero point zero five percent. [S3]
- `s07` **[Up to 50% slower]** Reaction times on some tests were up to fifty percent slower. You haven't had a single drink. Your brain just behaves like it's had a couple. [S3]
- `s08` And keep going a few more hours, and it got worse, as bad as the highest alcohol dose they tested. That's why you're not allowed anywhere near a car in this video. [S3]

## Hour 24: The First Cracks

- `s09` Twenty-four hours. A whole day. And deep in your brain, a small almond-shaped alarm system called the amygdala is getting louder.
- `s10` **[Amygdala: ~60% more reactive]** In one brain scan study, people who'd been kept awake for about a day and a half reacted to upsetting pictures with around sixty percent more amygdala activity than people who slept. [S6]
- `s11` Meanwhile, the part of your brain that normally says, calm down, it's just a toaster, has gone quiet. So everything feels personal. Even the toaster. [S6]
- `s12` And you're hungry. Weirdly hungry. Short sleep is linked to lower levels of leptin, the hormone that says you're full, and higher ghrelin, the hormone that says, feed me. [S7]
- `s13` That's why at three in the morning a salad has never once called your name, but cold pizza has.

## Day 2: Microsleeps

- `s14` **[MICROSLEEP]** Day two. Now the strangest thing starts happening. Your brain begins to fall asleep without asking you. [S9]
- `s15` **[A few seconds. No warning.]** These are called microsleeps. Tiny episodes of sleep that last a few seconds, sometimes up to fifteen. From the inside, it feels like nothing happened. The world just skips forward. [S9]
- `s16` At a desk, that's embarrassing. On a road, it's how a car travels the length of a football field with nobody really driving it. [S9]
- `s17` So your friends keep you moving. Basketball. Bowling. Anything. Because the moment you close your eyes, you are asleep. Instantly. [S2]
- `s18` Funny detail. Your basketball actually gets better. Partly because you're fit, and partly because you're playing basketball every night instead of sleeping. [S2]

## Day 3: Your Senses Go Weird

- `s19` Day three. Your senses start to glitch. The scientists test your taste, your smell, your hearing, and things are getting strange. [S2]
- `s20` **["Don't make me smell that."]** You keep begging them, don't make me smell that, I can't stand the smell. Ordinary smells now feel like an attack. [S2]
- `s21` And here's what your brain is quietly missing. In studies on mice, sleep opened up the spaces between brain cells by about sixty percent, letting fluid wash through and carry waste away. [S8]
- `s22` **[Waste piling up]** Awake, that cleaning crew works much more slowly. One of the things it normally clears is beta amyloid, a protein linked to Alzheimer's disease. You are now three days behind on the bins. [S8]

## Day 4: The Mind Cracks

- `s23` By day four, things get properly weird.
- `s24` **[Hallucinations. Paranoia.]** Lieutenant Commander John Ross, the navy doctor checking your health, later reported moodiness, trouble concentrating, short term memory problems, paranoia, and hallucinations. [S1]
- `s25` But the famous Stanford sleep scientist who joins the experiment, William Dement, sees something else. On day ten, you beat him at pinball. [S1]
- `s26` So which is it? Are you falling apart, or are you fine? The honest answer is, both. And the reason why is the weirdest part of this whole story.
- `s27` Your brain seems to be catnapping in pieces. Some parts asleep, other parts awake, taking turns. One of your friends later says your brain had been catnapping the entire time. [S2]
- `s28` It's not a superpower. It's damage control. You're not really awake anymore. You're just not completely asleep.

## Day 6: Your Body Fights Back

- `s29` Day six. Your immune system is running on fumes too. Sleep is when a lot of immune maintenance happens.
- `s30` In studies, people who skipped sleep the night after a vaccine often made a weaker immune response to it. Your body is too busy surviving tonight to plan for next month. [S13]
- `s31` And your memory? You're forgetting what you said a minute ago. You're losing the thread halfway through sentences. [S1]
- `s32` **[3rd biggest story in the US]** Meanwhile, outside, you've become famous. For a short time, your experiment is the third most written about story in the American press, behind the Kennedy assassination and the Beatles. [S2]

## What Kills You Without Sleep?

- `s33` So, day eight. Can this actually kill you? Here's where it gets dark.
- `s34` **[Rats: 11 to 32 days]** In the nineteen eighties, researchers kept rats awake on a turning disc above water. Every single rat died, or was put down because death looked close, within eleven to thirty-two days. [S10]
- `s35` **[Ate more. Lost weight.]** They ate more food than ever and still lost weight. Their bodies were burning energy at more than twice the normal rate. And the scientists couldn't find a clear cause of death. [S10]
- `s36` It took until twenty twenty to find a big clue. In fruit flies and mice, sleep loss caused a build up of harmful molecules called reactive oxygen species. Not in the brain. In the gut. [S11]
- `s37` **[Antioxidants = survived]** And when the scientists neutralized those molecules with antioxidants, the flies lived a normal lifespan with little to no sleep. Please do not try this with smoothies. [S11]
- `s38` In humans, there's a rare genetic disease called fatal familial insomnia, where the brain gradually loses the ability to sleep. People with it die, on average, around eighteen months after symptoms start. There is no known cure. [S12]
- `s39` Which is why, after people kept trying to beat your record, Guinness World Records stopped certifying sleep deprivation attempts. Too dangerous. [S2]

## Day 11: The Finish Line

- `s40` Day eleven. Your attention keeps slipping. One scientist later describes you as awake, but basically not working. [S1, S14]
- `s41` **[264 h 25 min]** And then, it's done. Eleven days and twenty-five minutes. Two hundred and sixty-four hours. The DJ's record is broken. [S1]
- `s42` **["Mind over matter."]** There's a press conference outside your parents' house. Someone asks how you did it. You say, it's just mind over matter. [S2]

## The Crash

- `s43` **[Slept: 14 h 40 min]** Then they take you to a naval hospital, wire up your head, and let you sleep. You sleep for about fourteen hours and forty minutes. And then you wake up, because you need the toilet. [S1, S2]
- `s44` **[REM rebound]** On that first night, the share of dream sleep, REM, shoots up. Your brain is paying back its debt, with interest. The next nights it settles back down to normal. [S2]
- `s45` And then, unbelievably, you get up and go back to high school.

## The Bill Arrives

- `s46` You seem fine. But decades later, Randy says the price came later. Years of awful insomnia. The boy who stayed awake for eleven days ended up struggling to sleep at all. [S2]
- `s47` Now look at your own week. Six hours a night feels normal for a lot of people. But in lab studies, sleeping six hours a night for two weeks built up deficits in attention and memory similar to one to three nights of no sleep at all. [S4, S5]
- `s48` **[You don't feel it. You just are it.]** And the worst part? People on six hours stopped feeling much sleepier after the first few days. The tests kept getting worse. They just stopped noticing. [S4]

## Outro

- `s49` So here's your prescription from this channel. Sleep tonight. It's the cheapest upgrade your body will ever get.
- `s50` And if you're watching this at three in the morning, I mean, I can't judge you. You're the one who clicked a sleep video instead of sleeping.
- `s51` **[NEXT: THE MARIANA TRENCH]** Next week, you fall into the deepest place on Earth, and we follow your body all the way down. Eleven kilometres of water, and one very bad swimming day. See you at the bottom.
