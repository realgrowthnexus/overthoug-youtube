# EP04 — What Happens to Your Body If You Eat Only Fast Food for 30 Days

_Your Vitals · narration script. Tags like [S3] link each claim to a source below._


## Hook

- `h01` **[DAY 30]** Day thirty. You've eaten fast food, and pretty much only fast food, for a month. [S1, S2]
- `h02` **[LIVER ENZYMES: UP]** Your liver is sending distress signals. In one study, liver blood tests jumped dramatically in under four weeks, in perfectly healthy young people. [S2]
- `h03` **[+6.4 kg]** You've gained around six kilos. That part you probably expected. [S2, S3]
- `h04` What you didn't expect is the part that shows up a year after you stop. Because a month of fast food leaves something behind. So let's do it properly, day by day, with your vitals on screen. Order up.

## The Rules

- `s01` You're not the first person to try this. In two thousand three, filmmaker Morgan Spurlock ate only McDonald's for thirty days for his documentary, Super Size Me. Three meals a day. Every item on the menu at least once. And if they offered to super size it, he said yes. [S1]
- `s02` **[~5,000 kcal a day]** That added up to around five thousand calories a day, and he cut his walking to about five thousand steps, roughly what a typical American walked. [S1]
- `s03` **[Linköping, Sweden, 2008]** But one man with a camera isn't science. So a few years later, doctors in Sweden ran a proper study. Eighteen healthy young adults, average age twenty six. At least two fast food meals a day. The goal, double their normal calories. [S2]
- `s04` **[Max 5,000 steps a day]** And no exercise. They had to stay under five thousand steps a day. They even had a matched control group living normally, for comparison. [S2, S3]
- `s05` So that's your challenge. Thirty days. Burgers, fries, fizzy drinks, and a couch. What could go wrong?

## Day 1

- `s06` Day one is fantastic. Obviously.
- `s07` **[Artery flexibility: halved]** But here's what's happening on the inside. In one experiment, a single nine hundred calorie, high fat meal cut how well an artery in the arm could widen by about half. Within two hours. [S4]
- `s08` It was still down four hours later. Your blood vessels need to be able to relax and widen. After a meal like this, they get stiff, for hours. Then you eat the next one. [S4]
- `s09` **[Blood fats: up]** In the same study, fat levels in the blood, called triglycerides, rose by more than half, two hours after that single meal. [S4]
- `s10` **[WHO limit: 5 g salt a day]** And then there's salt. The World Health Organization says adults should have less than five grams of salt a day. That's just under a teaspoon. Burger meals are not designed with teaspoons in mind. [S5]

## Day 3

- `s11` Day three. Meet the trillions of microbes living in your gut. They're about to have a very bad month.
- `s12` **[Diet changes your gut bugs fast]** In a study in the journal Nature, changing what people ate changed their gut bacteria within days. On a diet of mostly meat, fat and cheese, fibre-loving bacteria dropped, and bile-loving bacteria took over. [S6]
- `s13` One of the winners was a microbe that scientists have linked to inflammation in the gut. Your new gut residents love what you're eating. That's not a compliment. [S6]

## Day 5: The Crash

- `s14` Around day five, Spurlock reported feeling awful. Depressed. Tired. Headaches. [S1]
- `s15` **[Felt better after... a burger]** And the only thing that seemed to fix it? Eating another McDonald's meal. He'd feel better, for a while, and then crash again. [S1]
- `s16` Now, one honest note before we go on. Years later, in twenty seventeen, Spurlock admitted he'd also been drinking heavily during the experiment, which he hadn't disclosed in the film. So some of his results are messy. [S1]
- `s17` That's exactly why the Swedish study matters. Normal people. No secret drinking. Weekly blood tests. Let's follow them instead.

## Week 1 To 2: The Weight

- `s18` **[Week 1]** By the end of week one, the scale is moving. And it's not moving down.
- `s19` **[Spurlock: +4.5 kg in 5 days]** Spurlock put on around four and a half kilos in his first five days. [S1]
- `s20` And here's the thing about overeating. Your body can only burn so much. The rest gets stored, as fat, under your skin, and, more worryingly, inside your organs.
- `s21` **[Ultra-processed: +500 kcal/day]** Fast food also makes it easy to overeat without noticing. In a hospital study at the US National Institutes of Health, people offered ultra-processed meals ate around five hundred more calories a day than on unprocessed food, when they could eat as much as they liked. [S7]
- `s22` **[+0.9 kg in 2 weeks]** They gained almost a kilo in two weeks on the ultra-processed diet, and lost about the same on the unprocessed one. Same people. Different food. [S7]

## Week 3: The Liver Alarm

- `s23` **[ALT: way up]** Week three. Back in Sweden, the doctors check everyone's liver enzymes. Especially one called ALT. When liver cells are damaged or stressed, it leaks into the blood.
- `s24` **[Under 4 weeks]** And ALT levels rose dramatically. The researchers called it a rapid and profound elevation. In healthy people. In less than four weeks. [S2]
- `s25` **[Sugar = biggest link]** And the strongest link they found wasn't the burgers, exactly. It was sugar. How much sugar people ate in week three predicted how high their liver enzymes climbed. [S2]
- `s26` **[Liver fat: 1.1% → 2.8%]** Fat inside their livers more than doubled on average, from about one percent to almost three. [S2]
- `s27` Their advice to other doctors was blunt. If a patient has bad liver results, don't just ask about alcohol. Ask if they've recently been overeating. [S2]

## Day 30

- `s28` Day thirty. The finish line.
- `s29` **[Swedish group: +6.4 kg]** The Swedish volunteers gained six point four kilos on average, going from about sixty seven point six to seventy four kilos. In four weeks. [S2, S3]
- `s30` **[Spurlock: +11.1 kg]** Spurlock gained eleven point one kilos, about thirteen percent of his body weight. His cholesterol went up to two hundred and thirty. He also reported mood swings, sexual problems, and a fatty liver. [S1]
- `s31` **[IT'S OVER]** And then it's over. You go back to normal food. You start walking again. And the weight starts coming off.

## The Bill Arrives

- `s32` **[6 months later: -4.7 kg]** Six months later, the Swedish team checked in. Most of the weight was gone. Phew. [S3]
- `s33` **[1 year later: fat mass +1.4 kg]** A year later, they scanned everyone's bodies. Their muscle was back to where it started. But their body fat was still higher than before the experiment. About one point four kilos more. [S3]
- `s34` **[2.5 years: +3.1 kg (controls: +0.1)]** Two and a half years later, they were still about three kilos heavier than at the start. The control group, who never did the fast food month? Basically unchanged. [S3]
- `s35` The researchers said the extra fat was more than you'd expect from normal ageing, and raised the question of whether one short month of overeating could have a long term effect on body fat. [S3]
- `s36` It's a small study, eighteen people, so it's a question, not a final answer. But it's an uncomfortable one. One month. A souvenir that lasts years.

## Outro

- `s37` So, eat fast food for thirty days, and your blood vessels stiffen after every meal, your gut bugs get replaced, your liver sounds the alarm in under four weeks, and you might carry a little bit of that month for years.
- `s38` Does that mean never have a burger again? No. Everything in this video was about eating mostly fast food, way too much of it, while barely moving. One burger is a meal. Thirty days of them is a science experiment.
